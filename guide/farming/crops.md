# 🚜 Crop Guide

A pressed-and-labeled record of what grows in Fairhaven's soil, season by season. Remember the island's great mercy: seasons only change when the coven performs the ritual, so you may linger in any season as long as your ledger requires ([Farming 101](farming-101.md)). Just harvest everything before the ritual — out-of-season crops left in the ground vanish with the change.

> Specimens below are the ones this botanist has verified with her own trowel. Fairhaven's seed racks hold more than one journal page can press — treat these as the reliable core, not the complete flora.

---

## Spring Specimens

| Crop | Field notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Wheat** | The best coin of spring — about **16.9 coins per day** (roughly 135 per harvest). Bread begins here. |
| Turnip | The classic first sowing — quick, humble, dependable. |
| Potato | A sturdy staple; earthy, forgiving, and useful in many a kitchen recipe. |
| Cucumber | Crisp and cheerful; a good early companion for the pickle-minded. |

Spring is your tutorial season. Beyond wheat, profits are modest across the board, so favor whatever keeps beds full and the [Cooking](../crafting/cooking.md) pot supplied.

## Summer Specimens

| Crop | Field notes |
| --- | --- |
| Strawberry | The summer money-maker — roughly 23 coins of profit per day, the season's best earner. |
| Tomato | Kitchen royalty; more recipes want a tomato than you'd expect. |
| Corn | Tall, golden, and reliable. |
| Onion | A humble workhorse for the stockpot. |

| Green Beans | Thomas's other specialty seed; a dependable kitchen crop. |

## Fall & Winter Specimens

| Crop | Field notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Fennel** | The star of the cold ledger — grows in both fall and winter, and at roughly **24 coins per day** it is the most lucrative crop of the cold seasons. (Grown *out* of season under greenhouse glass it climbs to about 29.5/day — see the ledger below.) |
| Eggplant | A glossy autumn regular. |
| Carrot | Sweetens the fall table and several recipes besides. |
| Sweet Potato | Hearty autumn fare. |
| **Primrose** | The winter flower — and the most profitable flower in the entire game. Cold weather suits it. |

Winter's list is short, which is precisely why fennel and primrose deserve whole rows to themselves.

## The Profitability Ledger

### Cotton, the quiet champion

**Cotton is the most profitable crop in the game — about 39 coins per day — but only once you turn it into cloth.** You cannot sell raw cotton at all; woven into cloth, one seed packet's worth returns around 273 coins, and the fabric sells to Kai. Cotton seeds come from Thomas at his farm (as do green beans), not the general store. See [Artisan Goods](../crafting/artisan-goods.md) for the processing side of the ledger.

### Ranked by coin-per-day

1. **Cotton → cloth** (~39/day) — best overall, requires processing.
2. **Fennel** (~24/day) — best straight-from-the-bed crop of fall and winter.
3. **Strawberry** (~23.3/day) — best of summer.
4. **Wheat** (~16.9/day) — best of spring.
5. **Flowers:** **Primrose**, a winter bloom, is the most profitable flower in the game; **Sunflower** holds the summer title, with beehive dividends besides.

### Practical notes

- **The Greenhouse changes everything.** It's built through the **Greenhouse Affection** quest — deliver 50× Wood, 20× Iron Ingot, 18× Glass, and 5,000 coins to Parker Johnson. Once it stands, Lina stocks off-season seeds, and off-season produce sells at a premium: out-of-season fennel earns about **29.5 coins/day** and out-of-season strawberries about **28.7/day**. Since seasons only turn by ritual, you can simply *stay* in a profitable season while the greenhouse covers the rest.
- **Seeds:** crop seeds from Lina at the Dahl General Store, flower seeds from Violet, cotton and green beans from Thomas ([Shops](../island/shops.md)).
- **Selling isn't the only value.** Crops feed [Cooking](../crafting/cooking.md), [Potions](../witchcraft/potions.md), and gifts villagers adore ([Gift Preferences](../characters/gift-preferences.md)) — sometimes a carrot is worth more as a kindness.
- For the broader coin picture, see [Money-Making](../reference/money-making.md).

> Fruit trees are a separate investment entirely — one seedling, bought once, fruiting forever. Their page in this journal is [Beekeeping & Orchards](bees-and-orchards.md).
