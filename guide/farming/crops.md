# 🚜 Crop Guide

Fairhaven's growing calendar has one glorious quirk: **the seasons wait for
you**. Since the season only changes when Tara performs the ritual, "seasonal"
crops are really "as long as you like" crops. Plan accordingly — and
generously.

## What grows when

A pressed sampling of each season's staples:

| Season | Notable crops |
|---|---|
| **Spring** | Turnips, potatoes, cucumbers — quick, cheap confidence-builders |
| **Summer** | Onions, tomatoes, corn |
| **Fall**   | Eggplant, carrots, sweet potatoes |
| **Winter** | The hardy few — with fennel the standout earner of the cold months |
| **Any season** | Fruit trees — plant once, harvest all year round |

Two verified early benchmarks for your ledger: **turnips** grow in about
4 days and sell for around 60 coins; **potatoes** take about 7 days for
around 80. The in-game seed shop lists each crop's details at purchase — trust
the shelf tag over any almanac, ours included.

## Strategy for a player-turned calendar

- **Milk each season dry before turning the wheel.** There's no rush: finish
  crop collections, stockpile ingredients for out-of-season recipes, and
  *then* perform the ritual.
- **Harvest everything before a season change.** Out-of-season crops wilt the
  instant the ritual completes. The game asks for confirmation first — that's
  your cue to sweep the fields.
- **Buy seeds ahead.** Cooking recipes love out-of-season ingredients; a
  chest of last season's produce is a pantry, not clutter.
- **Fruit trees are the lazy witch's income.** Season-proof and
  ritual-proof — the only crops that shrug when the calendar turns.

## Making crops pay

Regrowing crops (harvest again and again from one planting) beat
single-harvest crops for coin *per bed per day* once a season stretches long —
and in Fairhaven, every season stretches long if you want it to. Fennel has a
particular reputation as the best coin of the colder seasons.

For where crops go after the harvest — juice, wine, dyes, dinner — see
[Artisan Goods](../crafting/artisan-goods.html) and
[Cooking & Recipes](../crafting/cooking.html). And do keep a bed or two of
**flowers**: they're not vanity, they're infrastructure — see
[Beekeeping & Orchards](bees-and-orchards.html).
