# 🌱 Beginner Tips

Cuttings from many gardens, rooted here for you. None of these are required — Fairhaven forgives everything — but each one will make your first season noticeably sweeter.

---

## Pace & progression

1. **Don't rush the seasons.** They change only when you perform the ritual, so there is *zero* time pressure. Wring every drop out of spring before you turn the page — and **harvest every crop first**, because out-of-season plantings vanish at the changeover. Details in [Energy, Time & Seasons](energy-time-seasons.md).
2. **Let the to-do quests lead.** The journal's quest list *is* the story and the unlock tree — new areas, crafting stations, and your entire magical education arrive through it. When aimless, open the journal and use a hint.
3. **Watch story scenes properly.** The game is fully voice-acted and the writing is the point. Skipping dialogue here is like pressing a flower without looking at it.
4. **Sleep before midnight.** Past the 12 AM warning lies collapse and a smaller energy meter tomorrow. Nothing you can do at 1 AM is worth it.

## People & the town

5. **Talk to everyone, every day.** Daily conversations are the cheapest, steadiest way to grow [friendship](../characters/friendship.md), and rising relationships unlock personal stories you'd otherwise never see. Make a town circuit part of your daily loop.
6. **Give gifts with intent.** Residents have likes and dislikes; a well-chosen gift outgrows a week of small talk. Consult [Gift Preferences](../characters/gift-preferences.md) as you learn them.
7. **Learn the shop hours early.** Nothing wilts a plan like arriving at a locked door. Our [Shops](../island/shops.md) page keeps the schedules pressed flat.

## Farm & field

8. **Leave some flowers unpicked.** Flowers still in bloom feed nearby **beehives**, speeding up honey and beeswax production. A picked flower is a one-time coin; a blooming one is an income. See [Bees & Orchards](../farming/bees-and-orchards.md).
9. **Water every day, harvest promptly.** The Water Pot is your first and truest tool. Harvested beds can be replanted immediately — keep the soil working.
10. **Upgrade your tools when offered.** Natalia's forge-work makes chores cost less energy, which effectively lengthens every single day for the rest of the game. Prioritize it over luxuries.
11. **Use the greenhouse for the long game.** Once available, it shelters growing things from the whims of the seasonal ritual — perfect for crops you don't want to gamble on.

## Money & materials

12. **Forage and fish from day one.** Wild finds and [fish](../farming/fishing.md) are free money that costs only energy and a pleasant walk. Early on, they'll out-earn your tiny fields. More schemes in [Money-Making](../reference/money-making.md).
13. **Don't sell everything.** Quests, [cooking](../crafting/cooking.md), and crafting constantly call for materials — wood, stone, produce, forage. Keep a modest reserve of common items before selling the surplus.
14. **Process before selling when you can.** Cooked and crafted goods generally sell better than raw ingredients, and [artisan goods](../crafting/artisan-goods.md) better still.

## Energy & witchery

15. **Carry food on expeditions.** A snack in the pocket turns a half-finished mining trip into a full one. Coffee from the diner is famously cheap and potent for its price.
16. **Spend the whole meter.** Ending the day with unspent energy is compost left in the bag. Even aimless foraging beats an early bedtime with a full tank.
17. **Keep the cauldron busy once it's yours.** [Potions](../witchcraft/potions.md) include energy restoratives, and coven tasks teach new [spells](../witchcraft/spells-and-rituals.md) — your night work feeds your day work, and vice versa.
18. **Weather is negotiable.** Later magic lets you nudge the skies; a rainy day, arranged in advance, waters the farm for you. See [Weather Magic](../witchcraft/weather-magic.md).

> 🌿 *Lore aside:* Fairhaven's older witches insist there's no such thing as a wasted day — only days spent on things that turned out to matter differently. The game agrees with them. So do we.
